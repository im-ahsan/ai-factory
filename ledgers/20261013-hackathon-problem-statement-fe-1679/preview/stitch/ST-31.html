Proposed Solution

Operating ledger for high-fidelity feedlot execution

Range Ledger replaces fragmented dry-erase boards, paper bunk sheets, and opaque spreadsheets with a single, permission-governed operations workspace for commercial cattle feedyards. Designed for Red Mesa Yard, the application unifies procurement intake, pen allocations, daily bunk deliveries, ration formulation, commodity balances, and cohort profitability into staff worklists grounded in continuous operational validation.

Real Product Description

Range Ledger is a desk-first and bunk-side feedlot management application engineered to handle the real-world friction of cattle operations: partial deliveries, allocation discrepancies, sensor telemetry interruptions, and strict ledger separation between physical bunk cleanout wastage and delivered ration consumption. It enforces transparent staff role governance so bunk drivers, feed planners, receiving coordinators, and yard managers execute synchronized tasks without silent data mutation.

Field Reality & Edge Cases

Operational challenges in feedlot operations

1. Stale head counts across pen shifts

Feed bunk deliveries are computed from head counts. When livestock move between pens or morbidity pulls occur without instant ledger sync, bunk plans risk overfeeding or underfeeding. Range Ledger decouples saved plans from uncommitted muster changes, highlighting discrepancies (e.g. Pen P-04's 142 saved vs 140 live count) until an authorized planner confirms regeneration.

2. Traceability conflicts in repeat procurement imports

When procurement documents like PO-8841 are updated after cattle have already been received into pens, naive software either duplicates records or overwrites physical reality. The Range Ledger adapter flags the 80 head source update against 82 mapped head, preventing silent alteration of animal ledger balances.

3. Strict separation of actual wastage from delivered feed

A cardinal failure in feed conversion accounting is conflating bunk cleanout spoil with consumed ration. Range Ledger isolates the 112 lb recorded physical bunk wastage from the 8,384 lb/day delivered feed ledger. Wastage informs procurement margin projections without corrupting true nutritional conversion ratios (FCR).
